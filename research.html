Behavioral diagnosis

“Forgetting” is not one problem.

The qualitative respondent surfaced multiple mechanisms. They are useful hypotheses, not population prevalence estimates.

Decision evolution

We kept the mechanism, not the first feature.

Contextual push reminders

Why attractive: high mobile reach.

Why deprioritized: requires predicting a shopping moment from incomplete routed-order history.

Desktop browser extension

Why attractive: intervenes after shopping context exists.

Why not lead: behaviorally strong but structurally reach-limited for mobile/app journeys.

Share / deep-link back

Why attractive: explicit intent and low persistent permission burden.

Why not lead: still depends on remembering CashKaro and taking an extra action.

Shopping agent

Why attractive: could move CashKaro earlier into discovery.

Why not V1: asks users to change starting behavior and requires data/integrations the core hypothesis does not need.

External-model red team

We used other models as critics, not authorities.

Qwen — useful

  • Reinforced the value / trust / context-friction hypothesis tree.
  • Preserved the randomized-user north star.
  • Suggested useful mechanism and guardrail metrics.

Qwen — rejected

  • Over-generalized N=1 evidence.
  • Invented adoption/uplift scenarios as if meaningful.
  • Recommended product-aware copy V1 cannot support.
  • Reintroduced AI without necessity.

DeepSeek — useful

  • Strong MVP discipline and ITT experiment design.
  • Separated CashKaro vs retailer incrementality.
  • Called out permission and partner approval as kill gates.

DeepSeek — rejected

  • Target cohort biased toward users already routing frequently.
  • Onboarding claimed knowledge of bypass purchases CashKaro does not have.
  • Arbitrary 8-second / 7-day / 30-day frequency rules presented too confidently.
